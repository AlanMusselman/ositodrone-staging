CHAPTER 02 • TARGET AGES 12–16

The Physics of Multirotor Flight — 4 Forces, 6-DOF & Torque Balance

An airplane needs a runway to move forward and push air over its wings. A quadcopter (multirotor) generates lift by spinning four miniature airfoils (propeller blades) at 15,000 to 35,000 RPM. To fly like an expert—and design your own airframes—you need to understand the exact physics happening every millisecond.

2.1 The Four Forces of Flight on a Quadcopter

Every aircraft in the universe is governed by four vector forces:

1. Weight (Gravity, W = m × g): Pulls the drone straight toward the center of the Earth. For an Osito CUB-75 MK-II with battery (All-Up Weight m = 39.5 grams = 0.0395 kg), Weight is 0.0395 kg × 9.81 m/s² = 0.387 Newtons.

2. Thrust (Upward Rotor Force, T): Generated as the four angled propeller blades accelerate a column of air downward (Newton's Third Law: every action has an equal and opposite reaction).

3. Lift & Tilt Vectoring: In a level hover, 100% of Thrust points straight up to balance Weight (T = W). When you push the right stick forward (Pitch), the rear motors spin faster than the front motors, tilting the thrust vector forward by angle θ. Now Vertical Lift is T × cos(θ) and Forward Propulsion is T × sin(θ)!

4. Aerodynamic Drag (D): Air resistance pushing against the frame and prop guards, proportional to the square of velocity (D ∝ v²).

2.2 Six Degrees of Freedom (6-DOF) & The Three Rotational Axes

In 3D space, a drone moves along three linear axes (Surge = forward/back, Sway = left/right, Heave = up/down) and rotates around three rotational axes measured by the flight controller's gyroscope:

• ROLL (Rotation around the Longitudinal Front-to-Back X-Axis): Controlled by Right Stick Left/Right. To roll right, the left motors (Motors 2 & 4) speed up while the right motors (Motors 1 & 3) slow down.

• PITCH (Rotation around the Lateral Side-to-Side Y-Axis): Controlled by Right Stick Up/Down. To pitch forward (nose down), the rear motors (Motors 1 & 2) speed up while the front motors (Motors 3 & 4) slow down.

• YAW (Rotation around the Vertical Z-Axis): Controlled by Left Stick Left/Right. How does a quadcopter spin its nose left or right without tilting? Through Conservation of Angular Momentum!

2.3 Why Diagonal Propellers Spin in Opposite Directions (CW vs. CCW)

If all four motors spun Clockwise (CW), Newton's Third Law dictates that the airframe itself would spin uncontrollably Counter-Clockwise (CCW) like a helicopter that lost its tail rotor! To cancel this reactive torque:

• Two diagonal motors spin Clockwise (CW), and the other two diagonal motors spin Counter-Clockwise (CCW).

• At Osito Drone Services, we configure our fleet in the 'Props-Out' (Reversed) direction: Front-Left (M4) and Rear-Right (M1) spin CW, while Front-Right (M3) and Rear-Left (M2) spin CCW. 'Props-Out' sweeps obstacles and ribbon gates away from the FPV camera lens rather than pulling them into the center of the frame.

• When you command a YAW rotation, the flight controller speeds up the two CW motors while slowing down the two CCW motors by the exact same amount. Total upward thrust stays constant (so you don't climb or drop), while net reactive torque rotates the nose smoothly!

💡 TEEN AEROSPACE FORMULA BOX: THRUST-TO-WEIGHT RATIO (T/W)

Thrust-to-Weight Ratio = Total Max Thrust (g) ÷ All-Up Weight (g).
• A drone needs T/W > 1.0 just to leave the ground, and T/W ≥ 2.0 for crisp control authority.
• Example: The Osito MiniCub65 BNF weighs 27.6g AUW and its four 0702 27,000KV motors produce 116g of peak thrust. Its T/W ratio is 116g ÷ 27.6g = 4.20 : 1—meaning it hovers at just ~24% throttle and has massive reserve power to recover from steep dives!

CHAPTER 04 • TARGET AGES 12–16

Avionics, Flight Controllers, PID Loops & Aerospace Firmware Safety

At the center of every Osito drone sits a 25.5 × 25.5 mm green-and-gold printed circuit board (PCB) weighing less than two nickels. Yet this tiny All-In-One (AIO) board packs five aerospace subsystems that used to fill an entire rack in a full-scale airliner.

4.1 Anatomy of the OSITO_CUBBRAIN65_V1 AIO Flight Controller

When you inspect an Osito flight controller under our bench magnifier, you'll identify five key chips:

1. Microcontroller Unit (MCU — STM32F411CEU6): A 32-bit ARM Cortex-M4 processor running at 100 MHz with a hardware Floating-Point Unit (FPU). It executes the Betaflight flight control loop 8,000 times per second.

2. Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU — TDK InvenSense ICM-42688-P): A 6-axis Micro-Electro-Mechanical System (MEMS) containing a 3-axis Gyroscope (measures angular rotation rate in degrees/sec) and a 3-axis Accelerometer (measures linear g-forces and gravity direction so the drone knows which way is 'level' in Angle Mode).

3. 4-in-1 Brushless Electronic Speed Controllers (ESCs — 12A Continuous, Bluejay 48kHz): Four pairs of half-bridge MOSFETs controlled by EFM8BB21 microcontrollers. Because brushed motors wear out after 6–10 hours, Osito uses 3-phase sensorless brushless motors driven via DShot300 digital telemetry and 48kHz PWM for extra flight efficiency.

4. ExpressLRS (ELRS) 2.4GHz Radio Receiver (Semtech SX1281): Receives stick commands from your Hall-gimbal radio at 250 packets per second (4ms latency) using LoRa spread-spectrum modulation that punches through gym Wi-Fi noise.

5. On-Screen Display (OSD — AT7456E) & 5.8GHz Video Transmitter (VTX): Overlays live battery voltage, flight timer, pilot callsign, and artificial horizon directly onto your FPV goggle video feed.

4.2 How the 8kHz PID Control Loop Keeps Your Drone Stable

Every 125 microseconds (8,000 Hz), the flight controller compares your Desired Stick Rate (Setpoint) against the Actual Gyro Rate (Measurement). The difference is the Error: e(t) = Setpoint − Gyro. The PID controller calculates motor corrections using three terms:

• P — Proportional (Present Error): P_out = Kp × e(t). Pushes back immediately in proportion to how far off-target the drone is. Too little P feels mushy; too much P causes fast, high-pitched oscillations.

• I — Integral (Past Accumulated Error): I_out = Ki × ∫ e(t) dt. Remembers steady forces like a crosswind or an off-center battery and builds up steady motor trim until the drift disappears.

• D — Derivative (Future Rate of Change): D_out = Kd × de(t)/dt. Acts like a shock absorber! As the drone snaps toward its target angle, the D-term brakes the rotation smoothly right before it overshoots (eliminating prop-wash bounce).

4.3 The Three Golden Rules of Osito Firmware & Avionics Safety

Across all 68 firmware checks in our workspace safety audit, every Osito script and flight controller enforces three non-negotiable aerospace safety protections:

• Rule 1 — Two-Stage Failsafe Watchdog: If the radio link drops or a companion computer hangs for >100 ms, Stage 1 auto-levels the drone at reduced descend throttle. If link loss exceeds 400–500 ms, Stage 2 disarms the ESCs immediately so a drone can never 'fly away.'

• Rule 2 — Pre-Arm Disarmed Safety Interlock: Flipping the Arm switch will NOT spin the propellers unless the throttle stick is simultaneously held all the way down (<1050 µs) and no boot-time switch fault is active.

• Rule 3 — 16-Bit Millisecond Integer Rollover Handling: Many microcontrollers track time using an unsigned 16-bit integer (uint16_t) counting milliseconds from 0 to 65,535 ms. At 65.536 seconds into a flight, the counter wraps around to 0! Naive code like `if (now_ms > timeout_target)` fails catastrophically at the 65.5-second mark. Proper aerospace code ALWAYS uses unsigned modular subtraction: `(uint16_t)(now_ms - last_ms) >= INTERVAL_MS`.

💡 TRY THE ROLLOVER MATH YOURSELF!

Suppose last_ms = 65500 and 80 milliseconds pass, so the 16-bit timer wraps past 65535 to now_ms = 44.
In Python or C: `(44 - 65500) & 0xFFFF = (-65456) & 65535 = 80 ms`!
Unsigned modular subtraction gives the exact 80 ms elapsed time across the wrap-around boundary with zero bugs.

5.2 1S LiHV Battery Chemistry, Voltage Thresholds & Energy Math

Our micro-drones use 1-Cell High-Voltage Lithium-Polymer (1S LiHV) batteries (300mAh to 450mAh capacity with BT2.0 high-current connectors). Unlike AA batteries, LiHV cells require strict voltage discipline:

• 4.35 V per cell = 100% Full Charge (LiHV peak voltage).

• 3.80 V per cell = Official Storage Voltage! Always charge or discharge cells to 3.80V on the ViFly Whoopstor V3 at the end of the day. Leaving LiPos at 4.35V for days causes internal gas generation ('puffing') and degrades capacity.

• 3.50 V under load = 'Land Now!' OSD warning floor.

• < 3.30 V = Permanent chemical damage to the lithium-cobalt-oxide cathode layers.

• Energy & C-Rating Formulas:

- Capacity in Amp-Hours (Ah) = mAh ÷ 1,000 (e.g., 300 mAh = 0.30 Ah).

- Stored Energy in Watt-Hours (Wh) = Nominal Voltage (3.8V) × Ah = 3.8V × 0.30Ah = 1.14 Wh.

- Max Safe Continuous Current (Amps) = Capacity (Ah) × C-Rating (e.g., 0.30 Ah × 75C = 22.5 Amps!).

💡 GOLDEN PIT CREW RULE: NEVER UNPLUG BY THE WIRES & ALWAYS USE A BAT-SAFE

Always grip the solid plastic BT2.0 connector housing when unplugging a battery—pulling on the silicone wires can tear the solder joint and cause a dead short! Always charge LiPo/LiHV packs inside a double-walled steel Bat-Safe box with a fiberglass flame-arresting filter, and immediately quarantine any dented or swollen cell.

6.1 How Optical Flow and 8×8 Time-of-Flight (ToF) Lasers Work

Indoors, GPS satellites cannot penetrate gymnasium roofs. So how does an autonomous Osito drone know where it is and avoid walls without GPS?

1. Downward Optical Flow Sensor (PMW3901): Works like a high-speed optical gaming mouse pointed at the floor! It takes 100+ tiny grayscale snapshots per second and tracks how floor textures shift between frames (Δx, Δy) to lock the drone in place with zero horizontal drift.

2. Multi-Zone Time-of-Flight Laser Ranging (STMicroelectronics VL53L5CX): Instead of ultrasonic ping sensors that bounce unpredictably off angled curtains, the VL53L5CX fires invisible, eye-safe 940nm VCSEL laser pulses and measures the exact picoseconds it takes for photons to bounce back across an 8×8 grid (64 independent distance zones at 60 Hz!).

    return (now_ms - last_ms) & UINT16_MASK

        and computes pitch/roll avoidance commands in real time.
        """
        # 0. Disarmed Interlock Guard: Never spin motors if disarmed!
        if not self.armed:
            return {"mode": "DISARMED_SAFE_LOCK", "pitch_deg": 0.0, "roll_deg": 0.0, "throttle_us": 1000}

        # 1. Two-Stage Rollover-Safe Watchdog Timer (100ms Soft / 500ms Hard)
        dt_ms = elapsed_u16_ms(now_u16_ms, self.last_heartbeat_u16)
        if dt_ms > HARD_WATCHDOG_MS:
            self.armed = False
            return {"mode": "FAILSAFE_STAGE2_HARD_DISARM", "pitch_deg": 0.0, "roll_deg": 0.0, "throttle_us": 1000}
        if dt_ms > SOFT_WATCHDOG_MS:
            return {"mode": "FAILSAFE_STAGE1_AUTO_DESCEND", "pitch_deg": 0.0, "roll_deg": 0.0, "throttle_us": 1150}

        # 2. Low-Voltage Battery Auto-Land Floor (<= 3.50V)
        if battery_v <= LAND_BATTERY_VOLTS:
            self.last_heartbeat_u16 = now_u16_ms & UINT16_MASK
            return {"mode": "LOW_BATTERY_AUTO_LAND", "pitch_deg": 0.0, "roll_deg": 0.0, "throttle_us": 1200}

        # 3. Inspect Center 4x4 Forward Laser Zones for Obstacles
        left_min = min(tof_8x8_grid_mm[r][c] for r in range(2, 6) for c in range(0, 4))
        right_min = min(tof_8x8_grid_mm[r][c] for r in range(2, 6) for c in range(4, 8))
        front_min = min(left_min, right_min)

        pitch_cmd = 3.0   # Default gentle forward cruise (+3 degrees pitch)
        roll_cmd = 0.0

        if front_min < MIN_SAFE_DISTANCE_MM:
            pitch_cmd = -5.0  # Pitch back to brake
            roll_cmd = +6.0 if left_min < right_min else -6.0
            mode = f"OBSTACLE_AVOID (Front={front_min}mm)"
        else:
            mode = "CRUISE_CLEAR"

        self.last_heartbeat_u16 = now_u16_ms & UINT16_MASK
        return {"mode": mode, "pitch_deg": pitch_cmd, "roll_deg": roll_cmd, "throttle_us": 1480}

6.3 Five Essential Betaflight 4.5.1 CLI Diagnostics Commands

When you connect an Osito drone to a laptop via USB-C and open the Betaflight Configurator CLI tab, these five commands let you diagnose any hardware issue in seconds:

• `status` — Displays MCU clock speed, CPU load %, gyro temperature, and active Arming Disable Flags (e.g., `THROTTLE`, `RXLOSS`, `CLI`—telling you why a drone refuses to arm).

• `tasks` — Shows real-time execution frequency (Hz) and microsecond jitter for the Gyro/PID loop (`8000 Hz`).

• `get small_angle` — Shows the maximum tilt angle allowed for arming (set `set small_angle = 180` on ducted whoops so you can use Turtle Mode / Flip-Over-After-Crash).

• `rxrange` — Verifies that your radio stick channels span cleanly from `1000` to `2000 µs` with center at `1500 µs`.

• `diff all` — Exports your customized configuration so you can back it up to a `.txt` file or flash 10 classroom drones identically.

💡 PRO DEBUGGING TIP: READING 'ARMING DISABLE FLAGS'

Remember: while you are inside the Betaflight `CLI` tab or connected to Configurator over USB, Betaflight automatically activates the `CLI` or `MSP` Arming Disable Flag so the props cannot accidentally spin on the bench! Always type `exit`, unplug USB, and place the drone on a flat surface before arming.

💡 PHYSICS IN ACTION: THERMAL EMISSIVITY (ε)

Not all surfaces radiate infrared heat equally! Matte roof shingles and asphalt have high Emissivity (ε ≈ 0.92–0.95), giving very accurate thermal readings. Airspace Law, FAA TRUST (Ages 12–16) & Your Road to FAA Part 107 (Age 16)

The moment your drone's propellers lift off the grass outdoors, you are operating an aircraft inside the United States National Airspace System (NAS). Knowing the rules separates an amateur from a respected Osito Cadet—and sets you up to pass your FAA Part 107 exam.

8.1 Step 1 (Today, Ages 12–16): Pass Your Free FAA TRUST Exam

Under 49 U.S.C. § 44809, EVERY recreational drone pilot in America—regardless of age or drone weight—must pass The Recreational UAS Safety Test (TRUST) before flying outdoors:

• Cost & Age Limit: 100% FREE, online, takes ~20 minutes, and has NO minimum age (12-to-16-year-olds take it at every Osito Academy camp!).

• Can You Fail? No! TRUST is an interactive learn-and-test module; if you miss a question, it explains the rule and lets you answer again immediately, issuing your official lifetime FAA TRUST Completion Certificate.

8.2 The 250-Gram (0.55 lb) Threshold, Registration & Standard Remote ID

Why did Osito engineer the MiniCub65 at 27.6g, the CUB-75 at 39.5g, the SHARD-75 at 45.5g, the OSITO-INT-95 at 101.9g, and the THERMO-95 at 192.5g? Because 250 grams (0.55 lbs) is the most important weight boundary in FAA law:

• Recreational Flights Under 250g (< 0.55 lbs): Exempt from FAA drone registration and exempt from broadcasting Standard Remote ID!

• Drones Weighing 250g or More (≥ 0.55 lbs), OR Any Drone Flown Commercially Under Part 107 (Regardless of Weight!): Must be registered on FAADroneZone ($5 for 3 years, registration number labeled on the exterior of the hull) and must comply with FAA Standard Remote ID (a digital 'license plate' broadcasting the drone's serial number, GPS position, altitude, and take-off location over Wi-Fi/Bluetooth).

8.3 Core Airspace Rules & How to Prepare for FAA Part 107 at Ages 14–16

Whether flying recreationally under TRUST or studying for your commercial Part 107 exam, memorize these core National Airspace System numbers:

• Maximum Altitude: 400 feet Above Ground Level (AGL) in uncontrolled Class G airspace. (Bonus Part 107 Rule: Commercial Part 107 pilots may fly higher than 400 ft AGL ONLY if within a 400-foot radius of a tall structure like a radio tower or building, up to 400 feet above that structure's top!).

• Maximum Groundspeed (Part 107): 87 knots (100 mph).

• Minimum Weather Visibility & Cloud Clearance (Part 107): At least 3 Statute Miles (3 SM) visibility from your control station, and stay at least 500 feet below clouds and 2,000 feet horizontally from clouds.

• Night Operations: Permitted only when your drone is equipped with lighted anti-collision strobes visible for at least 3 Statute Miles (flash rate sufficient to avoid a collision).

• Age 14 Exam / Age 16 Certificate Rule: At age 14 or 15, you can schedule and pass the 60-question FAA Part 107 Aeronautical Knowledge Test (`UAG`, minimum passing score 70% = 42/60) at an FAA testing center, and submit your IACRA application for your commercial Remote Pilot Certificate as soon as you turn 16!

💡 HOW TO DECODE AN AVIATION WEATHER REPORT (METAR) LIKE A PART 107 PILOT

Sample METAR: `KSFO 051856Z 28012G18KT 10SM FEW020 18/11 A3002`
• `KSFO` = San Francisco International Airport station.
• `051856Z` = 5th day of the month at 18:56 Zulu (UTC) time.
• `28012G18KT` = Wind blowing from 280° (West) at 12 knots, gusting to 18 knots.
• `10SM` = 10 Statute Miles visibility (exceeds the 3 SM Part 107 minimum!).
• `FEW020` = Few clouds at 2,000 feet AGL (add two zeros to `020`).
• `18/11` = Temperature 18°C, Dewpoint 11°C. `A3002` = Altimeter setting 30.02 inHg.

10.1 Three Worked Teen Aerospace Math & Engineering Challenges

CHALLENGE A — Thrust-to-Weight & Payload Budget (`CUB-75 MK-II` + `SNAP-CODE` Hat):

• Problem: An Osito CUB-75 MK-II weighs 29.0g dry and 39.5g All-Up Weight (AUW) with its standard 10.5g 1S LiHV battery. You snap on the 6.5g SNAP-CODE 8×8 ToF Laser Hat. Each of the four 0802 22,000KV motors generates 38.0g of maximum thrust. What is the new All-Up Weight (AUW), Total Thrust, Thrust-to-Weight Ratio (T/W), and approximate Hover Throttle %?

• Step-by-Step Solution:

1. New All-Up Weight (AUW) = 39.5g (standard AUW) + 6.5g (SNAP-CODE ToF hat) = 46.0 grams.

2. Total Max Thrust = 4 motors × 38.0g = 152.0 grams.

3. Thrust-to-Weight Ratio (T/W) = 152.0g ÷ 46.0g = 3.30 : 1 (Well above the 2.0:1 safe agility minimum!).

4. Approximate Hover Thrust % = 1 ÷ 3.30 ≈ 30.3% thrust.

CHALLENGE B — LiHV Battery Watt-Hours & Flight Endurance:

• Problem: Your CUB-75 hovers at an average current draw of 3.6 Amps using a 1S 450mAh (3.8V nominal) LiHV pack. To protect battery health, you use 80% of the pack's capacity before landing at 3.50V. How many Watt-Hours (Wh) does the battery store, and how many minutes of safe flight time do you get?

• Step-by-Step Solution:

1. Capacity in Amp-Hours = 450 mAh ÷ 1000 = 0.45 Ah.

2. Total Stored Energy = 3.8V × 0.45 Ah = 1.71 Wh.

3. Usable Capacity (80% Rule) = 0.45 Ah × 0.80 = 0.36 Ah.

4. Safe Flight Time = (0.36 Ah ÷ 3.6 Amps) × 60 min/hr = 0.10 hr × 60 = 6.0 minutes!

CHALLENGE C — Thermal Roof Inspection Timing & Delta-T (ΔT):

• Problem: During a post-sunset commercial warehouse inspection, the dry roof membrane measures 64°F (17.8°C), while a 12×12 ft section near an HVAC curb reads 79°F (26.1°C) on the THERMO-95 radiometric camera. Calculate ΔT and explain the physical cause.

• Step-by-Step Solution:

1. ΔT = 79°F − 64°F = +15.0°F (+8.3°C).

2. Physical Cause: Water has leaked under the membrane around the HVAC curb. Because water has a much higher specific heat capacity than dry rigid foam insulation, it retains daytime solar heat after sunset and radiates a +15°F warm thermal signature.
